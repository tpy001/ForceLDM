Motivation

Learning Latent Dynamics through Teacher–Student Distillation.

01

Adding force alone is not enough

When force is simply added as an input, rich visual cues can dominate the sparse force signals. The policy may still learn to copy demonstrated actions without understanding how contact will evolve, limiting its ability to handle new situations.

02

Predicting video can miss what matters for control

A realistic-looking video does not guarantee accurate contact dynamics. Predicting full frames also spends effort on backgrounds, appearance, and sensor noise. Adding force sensing to video models further requires substantial computation and synchronized training data.

03

Learn the motion and contact that matter

Optical flow directly describes how the robot and objects move, while force/torque signals reveal their contact interactions. Together, they capture complementary information for control. Action-guided distillation condenses these future signals into a compact representation, helping the policy learn what matters for choosing actions without reconstructing every sensory detail.

Figure 1. Comparison with prior methods.

(a) Force-aware VLAs incorporate force feedback but lack explicit modeling of future interaction dynamics. (b) Video-based WAMs model future dynamics through visual prediction, but often lack force/tactile feedback and may fit task-irrelevant visual details. (c) ForceLDM learns compact, action-relevant future dynamics by distilling future motion and force cues from a privileged teacher.

Method

Figure 2. Overview of ForceLDM.

(a) Both teacher and student generate actions from current multimodal observations and language instructions. The teacher additionally receives future force and motion tokens, and its future-aware representations are distilled into the student's future queries. (b) The future dynamics encoder converts future force/torque and optical flow into compact force and motion tokens. (c) Progressive noise injection perturbs the student's future queries with scheduled noise to improve generalization.

Training

A teacher with access to future force/torque and optical flow distills action-relevant dynamics into a student, while progressive noise injection improves generalization.

Inference

Teacher-free and future-free. Only the student branch and current observations are retained at deployment.

Key Findings

  • Future force and optical flow are complementary: force captures contact evolution, while optical flow captures action-relevant motion and alignment.
  • Distilling latent future dynamics is more effective than predicting future RGB observations for contact-rich control.
  • Progressive noise injection improves robustness when the student must infer future dynamics from current observations alone.
